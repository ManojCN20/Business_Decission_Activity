YOUR SIMPLE SPEAKING GUIDE

Better business decisions

A seven-minute talk connecting customer feedback, an operational change and an illustrative profit improvement. The only app control you need during the talk is Next.

What is the activity?

Show two fictional businesses. Ask people to choose one. Reveal costs, investigate customer feedback, explain a business change, show an assumed before-and-after result and ask the audience to choose again.

Before you begin

Open the app. Leave Talk length at 7 minutes. Read the “You say” and “What you do” notes once. Print this guide so you have your script while the projector shows the slides. Click Present to audience to hide the script. Move through the slides with Next or the right arrow key. You do not need a timer, forms, volunteers on stage or an AI account.

The activity, step by step

1. On slide 2, show monthly sales: A sells ₹10 lakh, B sells ₹6 lakh. Take hands for A, B or “I need more information”.

2. On slide 3, reveal direct costs and gross profit: A has ₹50,000 gross profit, B has ₹2.4 lakh. Ask whether anyone changes their choice.

3. On slide 4, give the audience 20 seconds to read the eight selected complaint messages. Ask which issue appears most often.

4. On slide 5, reveal delivery: 5, packaging: 2, billing: 1. Feedback gives a clue to investigate. The business should verify the cause and costs using operating records.

5. On slide 6, explain the fictional business change: test a more reliable courier and stronger packaging, then measure the result.

6. On slide 7, show the assumed monthly cost reduction. Sales stay at ₹10 lakh, direct costs fall to ₹7 lakh and gross profit becomes ₹3 lakh. Read the breakdown so people can see how the change affects the figures.

7. On slide 8, ask: “Which business would you choose now, and which evidence changed your view?” Accept A, B or more information. DailyCup still has the higher gross margin.

8. On slide 9, connect AI-assisted feedback grouping to verified data analysis. On slide 10, conclude: “Do not rely on gut feeling alone. Use data to test assumptions and make an informed decision.” On the final slide, explain how Millenial Events brings businesses and investors together and invite one useful introduction.

SLIDE 1 / ABOUT 15 SECONDS

Better business decisions

You say

“Good evening. I’m Manoj, a data analyst and AI engineer. We will choose between two fictional businesses, listen to customer feedback, and see how a business change could improve its numbers. I will ask for your choice again after we have more evidence.”

What you do

Introduce yourself. Explain that everyone can participate by raising a hand.

The businesses, feedback and improvement figures are fictional teaching examples. The opening photo is from a past event on the organiser’s website: https://millenniaevents.in/images/bengaluru/guests-group.webp
SLIDE 2 / ABOUT 40 SECONDS

Which business would you choose?

You say

“Here are two fictional coffee subscription businesses. BrewBox makes ten lakh rupees in monthly sales. DailyCup makes six lakh. Which founder would you meet first? Raise your hand for A, then B, or if you need more information. Could one person explain their choice in one sentence?”

What you do

Show only sales. Take a show of hands and one brief explanation. Then click Next.

Everyone can choose the same business. The activity still works.
SLIDE 3 / ABOUT 45 SECONDS

The costs change the picture

You say

“BrewBox has nine point five lakh in direct costs, leaving fifty thousand rupees of gross profit. DailyCup has three point six lakh in direct costs, leaving two point four lakh. Gross profit means sales minus direct costs, before other expenses. Who would change their choice now? Next, let’s investigate why BrewBox has such high costs.”

What you do

Read the two subtraction results. Take a second show of hands. Move straight into the feedback example.

DailyCup has higher gross profit here. We still need more evidence to assess an investment.
SLIDE 4 / ABOUT 50 SECONDS

What are BrewBox customers saying?

You say

“BrewBox shares eight selected complaint messages. Read them and tell me which issue appears most often: delivery, packaging or billing. Take twenty seconds to look. Which issue would you investigate first, and which messages support your answer?”

What you do

Give 20 seconds to read. Take a show of hands for delivery, packaging and billing. Invite one person to cite a message. Click Next to reveal the analysis.

These are eight selected fictional complaints, not a representative survey of all customers.
SLIDE 5 / ABOUT 30 SECONDS

The feedback points to delivery

You say

“Five messages mention delivery, two mention packaging and one mentions billing. Delivery is the most frequent theme in this sample. That gives us a place to investigate. It does not yet prove the cause or the cost. We should check order dates, damaged parcels and replacement dispatch records. The single billing complaint could also be serious.”

What you do

Read the counts and point to the supporting message IDs. Explain that feedback gives a clue, while operating records help verify it.

Five of eight means 62.5% of this selected sample, not 62.5% of all customers. The classification is a prepared answer key.
SLIDE 6 / ABOUT 35 SECONDS

A business change based on evidence

You say

“For our fictional example, assume the records confirm that delivery and packaging failures create expensive replacements and urgent dispatches. BrewBox tests a more reliable courier and stronger packaging. Normal delivery and packaging become a little more expensive. But suppose the cost of replacements and urgent dispatches falls. Let’s look at what those assumptions would mean in the monthly accounts.”

What you do

Explain the operational change. Make clear that the courier and packaging change the operation, while the data helps identify and evaluate the change.

Feedback analysis or AI alone does not increase profit. The change has to work, and the accounts must confirm the result. The coffee packaging image is an AI-generated illustration of the fictional case.
SLIDE 7 / ABOUT 55 SECONDS

The assumed result: higher gross profit

You say

“In this illustrative result, monthly sales stay at ten lakh rupees. Product and other direct costs stay at five point five lakh. Normal delivery and packaging rise from one lakh to one point two lakh. Replacement and urgent dispatch costs fall from three lakh to thirty thousand. Total direct costs therefore fall from nine point five lakh to seven lakh. Gross profit rises from fifty thousand rupees to three lakh. The net cost reduction is two point five lakh. These are fictional assumptions to explain the calculation, not a guaranteed result.”

What you do

Read the cost rows, then the gross profit row. Emphasise that sales stay the same and the profit increase comes from a net reduction in direct costs.

The assumed ₹2.7 lakh reduction in replacement costs, less ₹20,000 of extra delivery and packaging cost, gives ₹2.5 lakh more gross profit. A real business must verify costs in comparable periods.
SLIDE 8 / ABOUT 45 SECONDS

Which business would you choose now?

You say

“Now compare them again. In our scenario, BrewBox has ten lakh in sales and three lakh in gross profit. DailyCup still has six lakh in sales and two point four lakh in gross profit. Who would choose A now? Who would choose B? Who wants more information? What changed your mind? We have a stronger reason to investigate BrewBox, but these figures still do not establish an investment winner.”

What you do

Take a final show of hands and one explanation. Ask people to name the specific evidence behind their choice. Accept “I need more information”.

DailyCup still has a higher gross margin: 40% versus BrewBox’s 30%. Retention, cash needs, risk and valuation also matter. Do not force a winning answer.
SLIDE 9 / ABOUT 35 SECONDS

Where data and AI helped

You say

“AI can assist by grouping large amounts of customer feedback, and a person should check its classifications. Data analysis connects those clues with delivery records and actual costs. The founder chooses an operational change and checks the result. This application shows a prepared classification and an illustrative calculation. It does not run an AI model or show a measured client result.”

What you do

Connect your data analyst and AI engineer background to the example. Keep the explanation brief.

Explain the sequence: feedback suggests a problem, records verify it, a business change addresses it, and financial data checks the outcome.
SLIDE 10 / ABOUT 30 SECONDS

Business decisions need evidence

You say

“Our first choice used a first impression. Costs changed the picture. Customer feedback helped identify a problem to investigate, and the revised cost figures changed the comparison again. The message is simple: do not rely on gut feeling alone for business or investment decisions. Use data to test your assumptions, apply your judgement and review what happens. Now let’s connect that idea to the people in this room.”

What you do

Read the takeaway. Invite the room to think of one decision where better evidence would help.

Data supports judgement. It does not remove uncertainty or guarantee a successful business or investment.
SLIDE 11 / ABOUT 40 SECONDS

Where businesses meet investors

You say

“Good information helps us ask better questions. Millenial Events brings founders, investors and industry leaders into the same room so those conversations can begin. If you run a business, share what you are building, your progress and the support you need. If you are an investor, meet the founders, understand their business and ask about the evidence behind their plans. Curated introductions and founder conversations create an opportunity to explore whether there is a fit. Start with one useful introduction today. Thank you.”

What you do

Explain both directions of the connection. Invite businesses and investors to introduce themselves, share their interests and arrange a useful follow-up conversation.

If you have 5 minutes

Choose 5 minutes. Use the same eleven slides, take brief shows of hands and just one short audience explanation. Retain the feedback, cost breakdown and final choice.

If you have 10 minutes

Choose 10 minutes. Give the room longer to read feedback and explain their choices. Ask which further evidence they would need.

If everyone chooses the same business

Continue as planned. The activity demonstrates how evidence shapes a decision. It does not require disagreement or a change of vote.

If nobody responds

Say: “Higher sales might attract my attention. I would then check the costs and customer feedback.” Continue through the example.

The distinctions to explain accurately

Gross profit means sales minus direct costs, before other expenses. Feedback gives clues and needs verification. The improvement is a fictional assumed outcome. In the final comparison, BrewBox has more gross profit, but DailyCup has a higher gross margin: 40% versus 30%. Retention, cash needs, risk and valuation also matter.

Your conclusion

“Do not rely on gut feeling alone for business or investment decisions. Use data to test your assumptions, apply your judgement and review the result.”
